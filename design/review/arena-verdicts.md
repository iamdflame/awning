## Changes the loser needs

**pair-1 (A loses, awning storefront vs gelato poster)**
1. Break the conventional 50/50 split (headline left, device mock right). Let one element, the headline or the storefront, take over the frame at poster scale.
2. Replace the boxed UI mock with something bolder and more physical: a real storefront, a real product, or a larger illustrated scene. Right now the hero sits in a frame inside a frame.
3. Increase contrast and size in the lower half. The body copy, the small "PayBox plugin" footnote and the empty strip above the bottom bar all read as tentative.

**pair-2 (A loses narrowly to the hot-sauce poster)**
1. Push the headline to dominate the frame. It currently fills about 40% of the width, while the winner's type is edge to edge.
2. Pick one saturated, confident brand color field instead of cream plus green plus red plus brown. The palette is pleasant but timid.
3. Simplify the receipt and phone-chip overlays. Three floating cards compete with the main mock and make it busy without adding drama.

**pair-3 (tie, awning vs Wero)**
- For A: the same scale and color-commitment issues as pair-2. Bring one striking illustrative gesture to the level of Wero's hands.
- For B: fix the empty white CTA pill (its label is missing in the capture), add a visible nav or menu, and say what the product does in the first screen.

**pair-4 (B, awning, loses to JUICE)**
1. Commit to a typographic statement at the scale of the canvas. The headline is competent but at standard hero size.
2. Cut the secondary detail: the receipt, the phone chip and the tiny footnote. One idea, executed big.
3. Add a signature detail the eye remembers, such as a hand-drawn mark, a mono metadata layer or a live element, beyond the awning scallop.

**pair-5 (B, awning, loses to Osmo)**
1. Tighten the typographic rhythm: more refined tracking and leading on the headline, and a clearer hierarchy between body and microcopy.
2. Show the product as a set of real outputs (several storefronts fanned out, as Osmo fans its resources) rather than one boxed mock.
3. Reduce the chrome. The heavy green nav, the scallop band, the bottom bar and the framed mock make the hero feel enclosed. Give it air.

**pair-6 (A, Ridge Roast, loses to Savor)**
1. Replace the flat vector coffee bag with real photography or a far richer illustration: texture, light, steam, beans, hands.
2. Fix the composition. The headline column and the bag float in a cream field, with large dead areas at right and bottom. Scale the image up to bleed off the edges.
3. Refine the type. The slab is heavy and generic; pair it with a more distinctive secondary face and drop the pill-and-chip UI clutter.

**pair-7 (A, Spoke & Chain, loses to MANA)**
1. Deepen the illustration: one wheel and wrench is clip-art level next to MANA's layered, hand-made world. Build a full scene with depth.
2. Let the type and the illustration interlock or overlap instead of sitting in two separate columns.
3. Fill the right third and the lower half; the yellow field is empty below the buttons. Add product or service cues such as prices, a repair menu or photos.

**pair-8 (A, Barro, loses to Abel)**
1. Use real ceramics photography. A ceramics studio sells texture and craft, which flat vector pots cannot carry.
2. Rebalance the layout. The arch and text block sit small in the center with large empty margins and a heavy dead bottom band.
3. Retire the shared awning template. The scallop band does not fit a Lisbon ceramics studio; derive the motif from the brand, such as glaze drips, tiles or the wheel.

**pair-9 (tie, Saki vs Ridge Roast)**
- For A (Saki): remove or move the promo pop-up that covers the headline, raise text contrast on the busy video, and let the CTA read before the pop-up does.
- For B (Ridge Roast): see pair-6. Bring in real imagery, fix the dead space and add a distinctive type pairing.

**pair-10 (B, awning, loses to Aardvark)**
1. Add depth and dimensional imagery. Aardvark's 3D books and layered wave shapes give the page physicality, while awning's flat mock reads as a screenshot.
2. Increase the headline's scale and weight contrast relative to the frame.
3. Use a bolder field color or shape language to break the cream rectangle.

**pair-crowd-desktop (A, BizAgent, loses to awning)**
1. Drop the dark-purple gradient, gradient-text headline and three icon-card row. This is the default AI-SaaS skeleton.
2. Show the product (a storefront, a call, a payment) instead of emoji-icon feature cards.
3. Develop a brand: custom wordmark, an ownable palette and a typeface with character. Inter on near-black has none.

**phone-pair-1 / phone-pair-2 / pair-crowd-phone (A, awning phone, loses)**
1. The stylesheet fails to load on phone: the page renders as unstyled HTML (Times, blue underlined links, a broken logo image, a bare black circle). Fix this first. It is a blocker, not a design note.
2. Once styled, check the mobile hero order: headline, then storefront visual, then CTA, all inside the first screen.
3. Make sure the awning motif, the receipt and the phone-line elements reflow to a single column without overlapping.

**phone-pair-3 (B, Ridge Roast phone, loses to Savor)**
1. Replace the vector bag with photography; on a phone it fills half the screen and reads as clip art.
2. Reduce the awning band's height on mobile. It costs about 120px of prime first-screen space.
3. Add atmosphere, such as texture, a grain overlay or an image behind the type, so the screen is not flat cream.

**phone-pair-4 (A loses, blank screen)**
1. The first screen is blank except for a clipped cookie-policy line and a lime bar. The hero failed to render or is hidden behind the consent layer.
2. Do not let the consent banner block or replace the first paint.
3. Make sure hero media has a poster or fallback so something renders before scripts run.

**phone-pair-5 (B, Barro phone, loses to Abel)**
1. Use real photography of the work or studio. The illustrated arch is tidy but generic.
2. Pull the image above or beside the headline, so the first screen has a visual and not only text and buttons.
3. Give the wordmark and headline more personality than a default serif, and replace the awning with a brand-derived motif.

## Patterns

- **One template, five skins.** Ridge Roast, Spoke & Chain, Barro and the awning landing page share the same skeleton: scalloped awning band, slab or condensed headline on the left, flat vector object on the right, "Open today" chip, a primary and a phone pill, and a dark bottom bar. Each looks competent alone, but side by side they read as one template, and that sameness lowers their creativity score every time.
- **Illustration loses to photography.** In every pairing against a photographic site (Savor, Abel, Saki, La Boca), the flat vector hero lost on Design. The vectors are clean but lack texture, light and craft cues.
- **The awning page is solid but safe.** It is consistently around 6.4 to 6.5: a clear value proposition, good usability and a coherent metaphor. It beat the generic AI-SaaS page easily, but it lost or tied against every award-level reference because its layout is the standard split hero at standard scale.
- **Winners commit.** The references that won used one huge gesture: poster type (JUICE, Koffiracha, Aardvark), one saturated color field (La Boca, Wero, MANA) or full-bleed cinematic imagery (Savor, Abel).
- **Mobile is the critical failure.** The awning phone build renders unstyled (a CSS or asset loading failure), and one other phone capture is blank behind a cookie layer. These scored about 1 to 1.5 and must be fixed before any design iteration matters. The shop-template phone builds (Ridge, Spoke, Barro) are actually strong on usability (around 7.0) and only lose on imagery.
- **Reference sites have flaws too.** Wero shows an empty CTA pill, Saki's pop-up covers its headline, and Koffiracha has no visible navigation. Their usability scores reflect that, which keeps several pairs close.
