## Changes the loser needs (top three each)

**pair-1, B La Boca (desktop)**
1. Give the hero something to do beyond "order now": add one line of offer or location and a visible menu, not just a hamburger.
2. Fix the collision of the headline with the gelato tubs and cones. The type sits over the photo props and loses its edges. Give it a clean field or a deliberate overlap.
3. Bring the cropped "GLB" starburst sticker fully into the frame or remove it. Cut off at the bottom edge it reads as an accident.

**pair-2, A Koffiracha**
1. Add a visible navigation and a buy CTA on the first screen. Right now the only route forward is a scrolling marquee.
2. The bottom third is empty white under the lime swoosh. Carry the composition to the fold, for example with price, flavour notes or a "shop" anchor.
3. Tighten the bottle and headline interplay so that "SPICY" and "SWEET" stay legible. Masking eats key letters.

**pair-3, B iDEAL/Wero (desktop)**
1. The primary CTA pill renders empty, with no label. Fix the label render; as captured it looks broken.
2. Add navigation and an explainer line. The page states a change but gives no "what this means for me" on the first screen.
3. Trim the "Now in Phase 1" badge and the CC button clutter. The badge collides with the headline area.

**pair-4, B Juice**
1. Add a primary action, such as "See work" or "Start a project". The first screen is all wordmark with only a hamburger menu.
2. The upper-case grotesk body copy is generic next to the custom wordmark. Set it in a typeface with character, or at a scale that matches.
3. The mono microcopy (phone, time, tagline) is too small to read. Raise it to at least 12–13px equivalent.

**pair-5, A Osmo**
1. The flat grey field and centered headline read as a template. Give the background material, or let the resource cards dominate.
2. Make the tilted showcase cards larger and fewer so that the work is the hero, not the headline.
3. The pill-tag inline words ("Webflow", "HTML", "icons") add noise. Use one emphasis device.

**pair-6, A Aardvark Book Club**
1. The headline is set so tight that "talking" collides with the line above. Open the leading slightly.
2. The CTA is tiny and its pink competes weakly against the yellow. Scale it and pair it with a short offer, such as price or number of books.
3. The nav has too many equal-weight pills. Collapse it to 3–4 and separate the region switcher.

**pair-7, B Ridge Roast (desktop)**
1. The large dead zone between the menu card and the bag illustration leaves the composition unresolved. Enlarge the bag or bring in real product photography.
2. The flat bag illustration is too simple to carry a hero against photographic competitors. Add texture, lighting or a real-photo layer.
3. The menu card is cut off at the fold. Fit all three items, or crop deliberately so the cut is intended.

**pair-8, B Spoke & Chain (desktop)**
1. The price tags overlap the wheel and crowd the bottom. Give the tags their own band or reduce them to one hero offer.
2. The wheel and wrench illustration is clip-art-like. Increase its detail and craft, or use real shop photography.
3. The header "Open today…" microcopy and nav are too small and low-contrast on black.

**pair-9, A Barro (desktop)**
1. Three stacked white cards on a pale blue field read as a generic template. Integrate prices into the art direction instead of plain cards.
2. The page has large empty margins and no scale drama. Push the arch illustration and wordmark much bigger.
3. Bring the tile motif into the body, not just the header band, to build a real visual system.

**pair-10, A Saki**
1. The promo pop-up covers the headline ("Perfected" is overlapped). Never put a toast over the hero type.
2. The headline is set centered at the very bottom with weak contrast over a muddy video frame. Add a scrim or move it.
3. Give a primary CTA on the first screen. "Discover" lives only inside the dismissible pop-up.

**pair-crowd-desktop, A BizAgent**
1. Drop the default AI-SaaS skeleton: dark purple glow, gradient-word headline, "Powered by" pill and three emoji feature cards.
2. Build a brand: name, mark, typeface and colour with a point of view. Right now nothing is ownable.
3. Show the product (a real storefront, a call, a payment) instead of describing it in abstract claims.

**phone-pair-1, B La Boca (phone)**
1. There is no copy beyond the slogan. Add what, where and the price or hours under the headline.
2. The photo props are cropped at the edges in a way that looks accidental on narrow screens. Recompose for portrait.
3. The starburst sticker is cut off at the bottom again. Keep it in frame or drop it.

**phone-pair-2, B Wero (phone)**
1. About 30% of the first screen is empty gradient above the headline. Pull the headline up.
2. The CC button floats over content and steals attention from the scroll CTA.
3. Add one plain-language line explaining the change before asking users to scroll.

**phone-pair-3, B Savor (phone)**
1. The portrait crop of the hero video is a blurry, ambiguous close-up. Supply a portrait-specific crop where the food reads.
2. The "Watch — Episode" CTA is ambiguous for a product site. Add a shop or product action.
3. The top half carries no information. Move the headline higher or add a product cue.

**phone-pair-4, B (preloader only)**
1. The capture is broken. Only a preloader shows, and its counter reads "Loading 193%", which is a visible bug.
2. Cap the preloader time. Show content progressively instead of blocking the whole first screen.
3. Ensure the counter clamps at 100% and the page reveals on load completion.

**phone-pair-5, B Abel (phone)**
1. The hero video is upscaled and soft, and the label is blurry at phone resolution. Serve a sharp portrait asset.
2. The "SHOP TIBI" text link is too small and too weak as the only CTA. Make it a real button.
3. The logo overlaps the bottle cap. Separate the wordmark from the product, or use a scrim.

**pair-crowd-phone, B BizAgent (phone)**
1. Same template problem as desktop: a gradient headline, a glow and an emoji card. Replace it with an owned brand system.
2. The centered body paragraph at length is hard to read. Left-align it and cut it to one line.
3. Stacked CTAs of unequal width look unfinished. Unify their widths, or make the secondary a text link.

## Patterns

- **The house family (Awning, Ridge Roast, Spoke & Chain, Barro) wins mostly on Usability and Content, not Design.** On Design alone, Awning loses to Koffiracha, Juice, Wero, Aardvark and La Boca. The house sites win because the hero states the offer, shows prices and has clear CTAs, while many award sites hide navigation and give no action.
- **Desktop margins against real award sites are thin (0.06–0.35).** The house sites lose outright to photography-led work (Savor, Mana). Their flat vector illustration tops out around 6.5 on Design. The gap is imagery: no real photography, texture or lighting anywhere in the family.
- **The template is recognisable across the family:** an "Open today …" header with "Buy with an agent" and a "Call <name>" pill, a scalloped awning or pattern band, then a big name with a list of priced cards. Seen side by side, it reads as one skeleton re-skinned, which caps Creativity at around 6.
- **Barro is the weakest house member on desktop.** It is the most "document-like", with plain white cards on a flat field.
- **On phone the house sites travel better than the award sites.** Award sites suffer from portrait crops, empty space or preloaders, so the house family wins all five phone pairs, mostly on Usability.
- **The crowd baseline (BizAgent) is a textbook generic AI page (about 4.7–4.8).** Every house entry clears it by 1.5–2 points.
- **Broken or suspect captures:** phone-pair-4 B shows only a preloader with a "Loading 193%" bug. In pair-3 B (Wero desktop) the main CTA pill renders without a label.
